KaleidoEye: A Large-scale Dataset and Benchmark for Slippage-Robust Gaze Tracking in HMDs

Yingxi Li1, Xiaowei Bai2, Liang Xie2, Hao Su3, Zhuoru Li4, Feitian Zhang1, Ye Yan1, 2, and Erwei Yin2
1Peking University, 2Defense Innovation Institute, AMS, 3Zhengzhou University, 4Tianjin University
Code Dataset Paper Demo

KaleidoEye is the first large-scale gaze-tracking dataset with quantifiable 6DoF slippage annotations (comprising 3DoF rotation and 3DoF translation between the headset and the user's head). It includes over 1.75 million samples from 30 participants, providing synchronized binocular eye images, 2D/3D eyeball annotations, gaze coordinates, and 6DoF HMD poses. By precisely varying headset poses using a robotic arm, we construct 11,130 slippage conditions, narrowing the domain gap between experimental settings and real-world usage.

Abstract

Egocentric gaze tracking is essential for immersive interaction on head-mounted displays (HMDs). However, existing studies typically assume a fixed and tightly fitted headset placement, overlooking the inevitable HMD slippage under real-world conditions. Although several datasets with variable wearing positions have been proposed, the lack of diverse and annotated slippage hinders slippage-robust gaze tracking research.

In this paper, we present KaleidoEye, the first large-scale gaze tracking dataset with precisely quantified 6DoF slippage poses. It contains 1.75 million binocular images from 30 subjects, each annotated with eyeball parameters and gaze targets. By covering 11,130 slippage conditions, KaleidoEye captures extensive eye appearance distortions and gaze-space misalignments, enabling comprehensive studies of gaze-tracking under slippage.

Based on this dataset, we propose EST-Gaze, a lightweight and slippage robust gaze-tracking model for resource-constrained devices. By leveraging edge-guided spatial transformations, EST-Gaze learns to transform distorted eye inputs into a canonical view, improving visual consistency and gaze tracking accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate that KaleidoEye significantly enhances the robustness of existing gaze-tracking models under headset slippage. Moreover, EST-Gaze achieves an uncalibrated average error of 2.53° with real-time performance (40 FPS on HoloLens 2), outperforming state-of-the-art approaches.

💡 Introduction

💫 KaleidoEye is the first large-scale gaze-tracking dataset specifically crafted to capture diverse HMD slippage, with precisely annotated 6DoF headset poses to bridge the domain gap in real-world scenarios.

💫 Compared to existing datasets, KaleidoEye’s key advantages lie in the scale (over 1.75 million images) and its 11,130 robot-controlled slippage configurations, offering unprecedented coverage of headset displacements.

💫 This not only enables rigorous benchmarking of slippage-resilient gaze tracking but also underpins models like EST-Gaze, which transform distorted eye images into a unified canonical space for enhanced tracking accuracy.

📑 Existing Gaze Tracking Datasets

In ideal scenarios (left), the headset maintains a fixed spatial relationship with the user’s head, ensuring stable gaze-camera geometry. In contrast, real-world usage (right) involves inevitable HMD slippage, leading to unpredictable changes in eye-camera alignment and gaze projection, which significantly impairs tracking accuracy.
Traditional model-based gaze tracking relies on geometric modeling (e.g., PCCR) and requires repeated, time-consuming calibration to maintain accuracy under varying conditions. In contrast, appearance-based methods predict gaze directly from eye images using end-to-end learning, offering better adaptability and user convenience. However, their performance is inherently driven by the diversity and scale of training data—only large-scale, slippage-aware datasets can unlock their full potential in real-world scenarios.
Most existing datasets (e.g., OpenEDS) are collected under fixed and tightly fitted headsets, lacking HMD slippage and quantitative variability. Gaze models trained on such data generalize poorly when faced with slippage-induced appearance shifts in the target domain, resulting in increased tracking error due to an unaddressed domain gap.
Datasets like PVEye introduce slight variations in headset placement, capturing limited slippage conditions. While they improve model generalization to some extent, the slippage is neither diverse nor quantitatively annotated, leaving models still vulnerable to unseen real-world displacements.
Unlike prior datasets, KaleidoEye captures diverse and precisely controlled slippage conditions using a robotic platform, providing quantifiable supervision for model learning. This not only enhances slippage robustness and reduces domain gap but also facilitates interpretable modeling of gaze behavior in real-world AR/VR scenarios.

✨ Benchmark Methodology

🔒 Real-time gaze tracking on HMDs requires balancing accuracy and efficiency. While high-performance models are too computationally heavy for embedded use, lightweight models often fail to adapt to appearance distortions caused by HMD slippage.

🗝️ We introduce EST-Gaze, a high-performance and lightweight framework tailored for real-time gaze tracking on HMDs under slippage conditions.

🗝️ Unlike conventional lightweight models that struggle with appearance distortions, EST-Gaze leverages edge-based geometric cues to guide spatial transformation and image rectification.

🗝️ This slippage-aware correction enables the network to align distorted eye images to a canonical view, significantly improving gaze accuracy with minimal computational overhead.

Overview of the proposed EST-Gaze framework for slippage-robust gaze tracking. Given an eye image, we first extract a corresponding edge image using a multi-stage pipeline. Both the eye image and edge image are processed by separate encoders to produce feature maps, which are fused via a spatial attention module to generate edge weights that modulate the eye features. A pose-aware affine MLP estimates the transformation parameters, which are used by a grid sampler to produce a spatially aligned image. The aligned image is then forwarded to a gaze tracking network for prediction. By incorporating pose as an explicit modulation signal, EST-Gaze enhances the interpretability of affine transformation and captures the headset displacements.

📊 Comprehensive Evaluation of KaleidoEye

📏 Evaluation Metrics

We assess gaze tracking performance using the mean angular error (MAE), a standard metric that measures the average angular difference (in degrees) between predicted and ground-truth gaze vectors. A lower MAE indicates more accurate gaze tracking, with values closer to zero representing better performance. To ensure fair and comprehensive benchmarking, we use two representative backbone networks: NVGaze, a lightweight model optimized for efficiency, and ResNet-18, a widely adopted baseline for appearance-based gaze tracking.

📊 Model Evaluation on KaleidoEye

📏 Evaluation Metrics

To comprehensively evaluate gaze tracking performance under varying slippage conditions, we define a set of robust metrics that jointly measure accuracy, and efficiency. Specifically, we use Mean Angular Error (MAE) to assess prediction accuracy by quantifying the angular deviation between predicted and ground-truth 3D gaze vectors—lower values indicate better precision. Parameter count (Params) reflects model size and is critical for evaluating lightweight deployment potential. FLOPs (floating-point operations) estimate the computational cost of a forward pass and serve as an indicator of inference efficiency. Together, these metrics provide a balanced view of model robustness, accuracy, and practical usability in real-world AR/VR scenarios.

🎬 Conclusion

💥 We introduce KaleidoEye, the first large-scale dataset with 1.75M binocular samples across 11,130 quantified 6DoF slippage conditions for near-eye gaze tracking.

💪 We present EST-Gaze, a lightweight and interpretable framework that leverages edge-guided spatial transformations to normalize slippage-induced distortions.

🧩 EST-Gaze is designed as a plug-and-play module and improves slippage robustness by an average of 34% across diverse models—without sacrificing efficiency.

🔍 Our findings highlight a key insight: achieving real-world robustness requires both diverse slippage data and architecture-aware structural alignment.
